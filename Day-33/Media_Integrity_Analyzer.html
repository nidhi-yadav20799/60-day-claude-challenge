Welcome

Before you believe it, learn how to read it.

Every day, headlines and posts compete for your attention — and some of them bend the truth to get it. This short, hands-on lesson walks you through two real-world skills: spotting misleading headlines, and recognizing emotional manipulation. You won't be quizzed on facts. Instead, you'll look at real-feeling examples, make your own judgment call, then see the full breakdown.

How this works

3. Then we reveal the analysis, and your Media Integrity metrics update live on the right.

Challenge 1 · Headline Detective

Headlines are written to make you click — not always to inform you.

Editors and algorithms both reward attention. That pressure can turn a modest, nuanced finding into a headline that promises far more than the article delivers. This gap between headline and content is called a headline–article mismatch, and it's one of the most common tricks in modern media.

Why it matters

Most people only read the headline. If it exaggerates, that exaggeration becomes what you "know" — even if the article itself is careful and accurate.

Look out for: absolute words ("cure," "destroy," "secret"), emotionally loaded language, and claims that feel too dramatic for the actual topic.

    Challenge 2 · Emotion Detector

    If content makes you feel something fast, ask why.

    Strong emotion — fear, outrage, urgency, envy — makes us act before we think. Posts engineered to trigger a quick emotional reaction often use specific techniques: countdown timers, "us vs. them" language, fear appeals, or social comparison. These aren't accidents; they're designed to drive clicks, shares, or purchases.

    Why it matters

    Emotional content spreads faster and gets shared more than calm, factual content — which means the most manipulative posts often reach the widest audience.

    Try to notice: what emotion am I feeling right now, and which specific words caused it?
